# Frames that work and frames that backfire

## Weak and strong lines

| Weak | Why | Stronger |
|---|---|---|
| "We are not rioters." | Repeats their word | "We are nurses, drivers and pensioners from every town in the region." |
| "Fuel prices are rising." | No cause, no one to act | "The government chose to tax the drive to work while cutting the bus." |
| "The system is broken." | Too big to fix, no one to ask | "The minister can sign the freeze on Monday." |
| "Join the struggle against capital." | The base's words, not the middle's | "Help us make sure work pays enough to live here." |
| "Thousands will suffer." | Doom, no way out | "Thousands will lose their bus. Together we can keep it running." |
| "Studies show 37% of households…" | A number without a person | "Marie drives 40 minutes to her night shift. This tax costs her a day's pay a month. She's one of thousands." |
| "The elites don't care about us." | A vague, wide villain | "Three board members voted to close the clinic. Here are their names and their reasons." |

Naming a decision-maker for a decision taken in their public role is fair. Pointing at
their family, their home or their private life is not, and it turns the middle against
the campaign.

## The message box

For any hard question, prepare four boxes before talking to the press:

| What we say about us | What we say about them |
|---|---|
| What they say about us | What they say about themselves |

Then answer every question from the top-left box, and bridge back to it: "What matters
here is…". Know the bottom row so nothing surprises you; never repeat it.

## Testing

- Test with the persuadable middle. Supporters' reactions are useless: they like
  everything, and they like most the lines that put others off.
- Two versions, one difference, a count. Flyers with different sign-up links, two
  posts on the same day to similar audiences, two door scripts in two streets.
- Decide in advance what result picks the winner.
- Ten conversations with neutral people beat a hundred likes.

## Words the middle hears differently

Movement language often means something else to the middle. Check each key word: does
someone who's never been to a meeting hear what we mean? Watch especially for
abstractions (struggle, system, solidarity, neoliberal), acronyms, and words the other
side has already given a meaning.
