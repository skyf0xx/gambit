# Methods of nonviolent action

There are close to two hundred documented methods of nonviolent action. They fall into
three classes by what they do. Below are the families in each, with examples to draw
from. Pick by the job, not by habit.

## 1. Protest and persuasion

Show disagreement and numbers. Low risk, low leverage alone; the opening steps, and the
glue between bigger actions.

- **Formal statements**: open letters, declarations, signed public statements by a
  group (doctors, teachers, veterans), petitions handed over in person.
- **Wider audience**: leaflets, posters, banners, projections on buildings, newspapers,
  radio, posts, skywriting, messages on clothing.
- **Group representations**: delegations to the target's office, mock awards, lobbying
  in numbers, picketing an office.
- **Symbolic acts**: wearing a colour or symbol, returning medals or awards, prayer and
  worship, displaying portraits, symbolic sounds (pots, horns, bells at a set time),
  renaming a street informally, "lighting" a building.
- **Pressure on officials**: following officials to public events with a sign, vigils
  outside their public office, fraternising with police and soldiers.
- **Drama and music**: street theatre, satire, songs, humour stunts.
- **Processions**: marches, motorcades, pilgrimages, parades.
- **Honouring the dead**: political mourning, funerals as protest, naming victims.
- **Assemblies**: rallies, teach-ins, camouflaged meetings (a picnic, a football match).
- **Withdrawal**: walk-outs, silence, turning backs on an official.

## 2. Non-cooperation

Withdraw what the target needs. The leverage class.

**Social**: ostracism of officials in their public role (not refusing to sell bread to
their families), boycotting the target's events, staying home en masse, students
refusing to attend, withdrawing from a social institution.

**Economic: boycotts**: consumer boycotts of a brand or shop, refusal to rent, refusal
to pay rent or fees en masse, withdrawing bank deposits, refusing to pay debts or
interest, investor divestment, suppliers refusing a contract, international embargoes.

**Economic: strikes**: general strike, sympathy strike, go-slow, work-to-rule, sick-out,
refusal of overtime, strike by resignation, professional strike (lawyers, doctors
refusing a specific task), a one-hour stoppage.

**Political**: withholding allegiance, boycotting elections or a body, walking out of a
council or a consultation, refusal to carry out an order, slow and reluctant compliance,
civil disobedience of an unjust law, judges or officials refusing to apply a rule,
local government refusing to implement, withdrawal of diplomatic recognition.

## 3. Intervention

Disrupt the situation directly, or build the alternative. Highest leverage and risk.

- **Psychological**: fasting, self-exposure to the elements, a silent presence at
  every public appearance an official makes.
- **Physical**: sit-ins, stand-ins, pray-ins, blockades, occupations, nonviolent raids
  (walking onto a site to claim it), overloading a system (everyone applies for the same
  permit), nonviolent obstruction, guerrilla gardening, planting on contested land.
- **Social**: establishing new social patterns (eating together where it's banned),
  overloading facilities, speak-ins (standing up in a meeting), alternative social
  institutions.
- **Economic**: reverse strike (doing unpaid the work the target refuses to fund),
  stay-in strike, occupying land or buildings, alternative markets, alternative
  transport or services.
- **Political**: overloading administrative systems, seeking imprisonment
  deliberately, dual sovereignty and parallel government (a people's assembly, a
  citizens' referendum the campaign runs itself).

## Concentration and dispersion

| Kind | Concentrated | Dispersed |
|---|---|---|
| Examples | march, occupation, blockade | stay-home, boycott, pot-banging, window signs |
| Visibility | high, one image | lower, needs a count |
| Repression | easy to contain or charge | hard: there's nowhere to send the police |
| Who can join | the fit and the brave | everyone, families included |
| Best when | numbers are high, policing restrained | policing is hostile, or turnout is falling |

## Designing a dilemma action

1. Find what the target has banned, refused or neglected that people widely think is
   reasonable.
2. Do it openly, in a form that's hard to call a threat: food, water, names, flowers,
   a lesson, a song.
3. Announce it in advance, so the target must decide in public.
4. Film everything.
5. Have the story ready both ways: "they let us, so…" and "they stopped us, so…".

Examples of the pattern: marching to the sea to make banned salt; serving free meals in
a park where feeding the homeless was banned; reading the names of the dead outside a
ministry; teaching a lesson on the steps of a closed school; planting a garden on land
earmarked for sale.

## Escalation ladder

A campaign typically climbs:

1. Ask (letter, meeting request) — and publish the answer.
2. Show numbers (petition, rally) — and set a date for a reply.
3. Symbolic pressure on the target in their public role.
4. Non-cooperation by the group with most leverage.
5. Wider non-cooperation, dispersed.
6. Intervention, with training and legal support in place.

Climb only as far as needed, and only after a structure test shows the people are
there (see `organizing`).
