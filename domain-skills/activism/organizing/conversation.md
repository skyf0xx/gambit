# The organizing conversation

A one-to-one, ideally in person, 20 to 40 minutes. The organiser talks about a third of
the time. The goal is not to persuade but to help someone see their own reasons to act,
and then to ask them to.

## The steps

1. **Opening.** Who you are, why you're here, in one or two lines. "I live in Block B.
   A few of us are talking to everyone about the rent rise."
2. **Their issues.** Ask, then listen. "How's the rise hitting you?" "What else is
   hard about living here?" Follow what they care about, even if it's not the
   campaign's issue. Write it down afterwards.
3. **Agitation.** Help them look at it straight. "How long has that been going on?"
   "Who decided that?" "How does it make you feel?" "What happens if nothing changes?"
   Not stirring anger for its own sake: helping them stop accepting what they've
   learned to put up with.
4. **Vision.** "What would it look like if this were fixed?" "What would it take?"
   Then the campaign, in a sentence, as a way to get there together.
5. **The ask.** Concrete, small, now. "Will you sign the pledge?" "Will you come on
   Thursday?" "Will you talk to the two neighbours on your landing?" Ask, then be quiet
   and let them answer.
6. **Inoculation.** Tell them what the other side will do, before it happens. "When
   they hear about this, they'll say the rise is legal and there's nothing to be done.
   Some people will get a letter about their tenancy. Here's why that's a scare tactic,
   and here's what we do if it happens." A threat people were warned about loses most
   of its force.
7. **Follow-up.** Agree when you'll next talk. Rate them on the chart by what they did,
   not what they said.

## Handling answers

- **"I'm too busy."** Ask what takes their time; often that's an issue. Make the ask
  smaller.
- **"It won't change anything."** Agree that alone it won't. Ask what would make them
  believe it could: numbers, a win elsewhere, a leader they trust being in.
- **"I don't want trouble."** Take the fear seriously. Say what the risks are and how
  the group protects people. Offer a lower-risk role.
- **"I agree with you" (and nothing more).** That's a 3, not a 2. Make the ask anyway.
- **A hard no.** Thank them, leave on good terms, rate them, move on. Don't argue.

## The public story

For recruiting talks and meetings, three parts, in a few minutes:

- **Self**: a specific moment when the speaker was called to this. Not a CV: a choice
  they faced, and what they did.
- **Us**: what the people listening share: a place, an experience, values. Specific
  people and moments, not slogans.
- **Now**: why this can't wait, the choice in front of us, and the one thing to do
  tonight.

A story told as a moment ("the morning the letter came") moves people; a story told as
a summary ("I've always cared about housing") doesn't.
