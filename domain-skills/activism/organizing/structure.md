# Charting and structure

## The chart

A chart is a list of everyone in the constituency, by unit, with a rating, a note of
who has talked to them, and when. It's the campaign's real map.

| Unit | Name | Rating | Talked to by | Date | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Block C, floor 2 | M. Diallo | 1 | — | — | Others ask her about repairs |
| Block C, floor 2 | J. Petit | 3 | M. Diallo | 4 Mar | Worried about eviction |

Ratings:

1. **Leader**: has followers, and is with us.
2. **Supporter**: will act when asked.
3. **Undecided / unknown**: not yet talked to, or unsure.
4. **Leaning against**.
5. **Opposed**, or close to the target.

Rules of thumb:

- Rate on what people have done, not what they said. Signed, came, brought someone:
  that's a 2. Nodded at the door is a 3.
- A unit with no 1 in it is unorganized, however many 2s it has.
- Re-rate after every structure test. Ratings drift up in the organisers' heads.
- Keep the chart where it's safe. It's sensitive: it says who supports what.

Large constituencies (a town, a region) chart by unit, with a contact person per unit,
and each unit keeps its own chart.

## Structure tests

A structure test is a public action whose real purpose is to measure, and build,
commitment before a riskier step.

| Step | Asks of people | Shows |
|---|---|---|
| Petition | a signature | who agrees |
| Public pledge | a name or photo, public | who will be seen agreeing |
| Delegation | an hour, in person, to the target | who will act in front of power |
| Short action | a stoppage, a stay-home, a turnout | who will take a small risk |
| The escalation | a strike, a rent strike, an occupation | — |

Set the threshold before each step. Typical: a clear majority of the constituency for a
step that depends on unity (a strike fails badly at 40%), and enough for the action to
be safe for those who take part. A missed test is good news early: it says where to
organize, before a failed escalation says it in public.

## Teams and leaders

- **Teams** of five to ten, each with a leader and a clear piece: a street, a shift, a
  floor, a task (media, legal, welfare, food).
- **Leaders recruit leaders.** A leader's first job is to find and coach the next one.
  An organiser who does everything personally has built a dependency, not a structure.
- **Coordinating team** of team leaders, meeting on a fixed rhythm.
- **Spokespeople**: several, chosen by the members, rotating, briefed together.
- **Decisions**: who decides what, written down. Movements that decide everything in
  open assemblies decide slowly and can be swamped; ones decided by a few lose trust.

## Meetings that build

- Personal invitation beats announcement. Expect about half of those who say yes to
  come; remind them the day before and the hour before.
- Start on time, end on time, under 90 minutes.
- Every agenda ends with commitments, written down: who, what, by when.
- New people get a job at their first meeting, however small.
- The next meeting's date is set before people leave.

## Warning signs

- The same people at every action, with fewer each time.
- No one can say how many people the campaign has.
- One person holds all the contacts.
- Decisions are made by whoever stays latest.
- New people come once and don't come back.
