# The issue test

A campaign issue is chosen, not found. Score a candidate against each line; a demand
that fails one of the first five rarely survives contact.

| Test | Question to ask | Fails when |
|---|---|---|
| Winnable | Can the people involved see how this could be won, with what they have? | Only a revolution, an election landslide or a miracle grants it |
| Widely felt | Do many people in the constituency share it? | It matters to the activists, not the neighbours |
| Deeply felt | Do they care enough to give up an evening, a day's pay, a risk? | People agree, and nobody comes |
| Clear target | Is there one person who can say yes? | The answer is "society", "the system", or a body with no one in charge |
| Clear time frame | Is there a date it gets decided by, and does it work for us? | The decision is years off, or falls in August when everyone is away |
| Builds the organization | Will fighting it bring in members, leaders, money? | It can be won by three people writing letters |
| Real improvement | Will it make a visible difference in people's lives? | It's symbolic only, and the base knows it |
| Shifts power | Will winning give the group standing for the next fight? | It's a one-off favour granted to make the group go away |
| Non-divisive | Does the base agree on it? | It splits the coalition along a line that matters to them |
| Easy to say | Can it be said in one sentence a stranger understands? | It needs a briefing paper to explain |
| Sets up the next one | Does it lead to a bigger demand? | It's a dead end even when won |
| Consistent with values | Is it something the group is proud to fight for? | It wins by asking for something the members don't believe in |

## Problem → issue: a worked example

Problem: "Living in this town costs too much."

- Too broad. Narrow by who is hurt most and what one person decides.
- "Rents in the council's own housing went up 9% this year." Still a problem.
- "The council leader cancels the 9% rise in council rents at the February budget
  meeting." An issue: one person, one decision, one date, and every tenant feels it.
- Short-term win on the way: "The housing committee chair meets a tenants' delegation in
  public before the budget is drafted." Winnable in weeks, builds the tenants'
  association, and puts the target on record.
- Long-term goal it serves: "Housing costs in town no more than a third of income."

## Many demands, one lead

A movement that grows fast collects demands from everyone who joins. That's a strength
for recruiting and a weakness in the fight. Signs it has become a weakness:

- Members disagree in public about what the movement wants.
- The target answers one small demand and the press reports the matter as settled.
- No one can say what would make the movement stop.

The fix is not to drop the platform but to rank it: one lead demand the whole movement
pushes now, the next one or two queued, the rest kept as the long-term programme. Pick
the lead by the issue test, and by what most of the members would choose if asked. A
demand everyone already agrees on (a unity demand) often beats a more ambitious one
that splits the base.

## Process instead of substance

The target's cheapest answer to pressure is a process: a consultation, a commission,
a review, a citizens' debate, a working group. Some are real. Most work by spending the
campaign's energy and the news cycle. Tests:

- Is there a date by which a decision gets made?
- Is the target publicly committed to act on the outcome?
- Who sits on it, and who chose them?
- Does the campaign keep its own voice, or speak only through the process?

Two noes: treat it as a delay, say so publicly, and keep the pressure on.
