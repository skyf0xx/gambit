# Positioning

## 01 — Category

- **Stated category:** AI strategy advisor
- **Actual category:** What people compare Gambit against is "ask ChatGPT," "hire a coach" and "make a Notion plan."
- **Opportunity category:** **A notebook that thinks back.** A strategist's working notebook for one hard goal. It combines conversation (what chat does well) with a durable written record (what notebooks do well) and adds real planning methods, which neither has.

Recommendation: position against general chat, and borrow the notebook as the familiar frame. "AI strategist" sounds corporate and inflated to this audience. "Notebook" sounds personal, private and useful.

## 02 — Landscape

Axes: **Forgets ← → Keeps a record** and **Agreeable ← → Candid**

- **General chat (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini):** agreeable, and mostly forgets. Memory features store facts but not a living plan. Easy to reach, which makes it the one to beat.
- **AI life coaches and coaching apps:** agreeable, partly keeps a record. Motivational, with streaks and check-ins, and usually a subscription.
- **Human coaches and mentors:** candid, keep a record, but cost hundreds per month or depend on who you know.
- **Notion templates and planners:** keep a record, but aren't agreeable or candid. They're silent and don't think.
- **Gambit:** candid and keeps a record. It's the empty corner, a sharp mentor's honesty in a notebook that remembers.

## 03 — Territory

**The space:** Honest strategic thinking, written down.

**The audience owned:** People working on one hard, self-directed goal who are tired of advice that flatters and forgets.

**The moat:**
- Eighteen methods drawn from operational planning, negotiation, forecasting and risk, wired to one goal record that each method updates in place.
- Local-first privacy that a hosted, venture-funded competitor can't easily match without changing its business model.
- Open source, so the method is inspectable and trust is earned in public.

## 04 — Positioning statement

**Standard:** For people chasing a hard goal on their own, Gambit is the strategy notebook that thinks back. It pressure-tests the plan, keeps a written record of where things stand and always ends on the next move, using methods professional planners use, with everything kept private on your device.

**Internal:** Gambit wins users from general-purpose chat by combining candid, method-driven reasoning with a persistent goal record, and wins trust through local-first, open-source architecture.

**Public:** Think it through with someone who tells you the truth and writes it down.

## 05 — Proof points

- **Methods, not vibes:** premortems, red-teaming, stakeholder mapping, negotiation prep, dated forecasts and after-action reviews. Each is a published skill in the repo.
- **It keeps the page:** every session writes to one living page of the plan, risks, decisions and progress, and the page is the first thing you see.
- **It pushes back:** the guided-session rules require pressure-testing before anything gets committed.
- **Private by construction:** no account, no server, and data stays in the browser. The build locks down network calls, and the code is there to read.
- **Free:** open source. You pay your model provider directly, often a few dollars a month.

## 06 — What Gambit refuses to be

- Gambit is not for people who want to be told they're doing great.
- Gambit won't compete on number of features, integrations or team collaboration.
- Gambit doesn't track habits, streaks or productivity scores.
- Gambit never uses military or chess jargon in its pitch. The methods are the engine, not the paint job.

## 07 — One sentence

Gambit is a free, private strategy notebook for people working on a hard goal: it thinks with you, pushes back and keeps track of what happens.

## Taglines

- **Think it through. Write it down. Make the move.** The whole product loop in nine words.
- **The notebook that thinks back.** Owns the category idea. Best as a subtitle.
- **Honest strategy for hard goals.** Plain and literal. Good for SEO and GitHub.
- **What's your next move?** Uses the chess meaning of the name without chess clichés. Works as the empty-state prompt.

Recommended: **"The notebook that thinks back"** as the brand line, with **"What's your next move?"** as the product's recurring question.

## Red flags in current copy (README)

- "Use Gambit for almost anything" and "Anything you can imagine" is too broad to own. Name three real goals instead.
- "Get 10X better outcomes" is an unfalsifiable claim that the brand's own rules reject.
- "G A M B I T ⭐ A.I." reads as hype and fights the notebook tone.
