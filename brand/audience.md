# Target Audience

## 01 — Audience overview

Gambit is for people carrying one hard goal that nobody hands them a plan for. That could be growing a business, starting one, leaving a career, moving a family or leading a campaign. What they share is the situation, not the job: the stakes are real, the path is open-ended, and there's no boss, board or coach to think it through with. Most have tried planning with a chatbot and found it agreeable and forgetful. They want someone sharp and honest to think with, and a page that shows where things stand.

## 02 — Primary audience profile

**Demographics**
- Age 25–55
- Mixed gender, English-speaking first
- Every income level. Many are spending their own money on the goal, so cost matters.
- Every level of technical comfort. Most have never seen an API key.
- Some work on the goal full time (owners, founders). Others fit it around a job, family or caring for someone.

**Psychographics**
- Values honesty over reassurance, and would rather hear the problem now than later
- Is used to being capable, and frustrated that this goal won't behave
- Has a notebook, notes app or spreadsheet full of half-plans
- Hesitates to put money, family or employer details into a cloud tool

**Behavior**
- Trigger: a moment of commitment or a stall. A lease is up for renewal, a resignation letter is drafted, savings are running down, or nothing has moved in months.
- Discovery comes from a friend, a community for their goal (a business owners' group, an organizing network, a subreddit), a newsletter or a shared link
- Decides in the first five minutes. Leaves at the first confusing step.

## 03 — Personas

The personas differ where it matters: what's at stake, how technical they are and which parts of Gambit they lean on.

### Rosa, 44 — owns two hair salons, Birmingham

*"Everyone tells me to open a third one. Nobody's asked if I can survive it."*

**Their world:** Rosa built the business over twelve years, employs nine people and does the books on Sunday nights. A landlord has offered a third site on a ten-year lease, and the offer expires in six weeks.

**Goals:** Make the call on the third site with clear eyes. Keep the two existing salons healthy. Stop being the bottleneck for every decision.

**Frustrations:** Business advice written for startups or corporations. An accountant who answers the numbers question but not the whether question. No time to learn a new tool.

**Needs from Gambit:** An honest read on capacity and risk, and prep for the lease negotiation.

**Decision triggers:** Recommended by another owner. Leaves if setup takes more than a few minutes or feels technical.

**Channels:** Local business networks, trade associations, Facebook groups, Instagram

**How Gambit helps:** It checks the real hours, cash and management capacity behind a third site, records the decision along with what would reverse it and preps the landlord conversation with a walk-away point.

### Kwame, 29 — founder of a meal-prep subscription, eight months in, Lagos

*"I have nine months of runway and forty ideas. I need to know which three are real."*

**Their world:** Kwame left consulting to start the business on savings and has 60 paying customers, one part-time helper and a spreadsheet of growth ideas nobody has tested.

**Goals:** Reach 200 subscribers before the money runs out. Know when to hire. Stop guessing about what works.

**Frustrations:** Startup content that assumes Silicon Valley funding. Advisors with opinions and no time. Planning that never reaches a real test.

**Needs from Gambit:** Cheap tests with the pass mark set in advance, dated predictions to keep them honest, and a map of who to win over.

**Decision triggers:** Wants something free that respects their data. Comfortable enough to get an API key once shown how.

**Channels:** Founder WhatsApp groups, X, LinkedIn, accelerator communities, podcasts

**How Gambit helps:** It turns forty ideas into three experiments with thresholds set in advance, forecasts the subscriber curve and checks the real hours before a first hire.

### Ines, 37 — cofounder and CEO of a clinic-scheduling startup, raising a Series A, Austin

*"Every investor meeting teaches me something. I just can't hold all of it in my head at once."*

**Their world:** Ines runs a 14-person company with 11 months of runway, a lead investor from the seed round who's lukewarm on leading again, and a board meeting in five weeks. Their calendar is forty partner meetings, each with different feedback.

**Goals:** Close the round in the next four months on terms that keep the founders in control. Keep the team calm and shipping while it happens. Have a plan if the round doesn't come together.

**Frustrations:** Advice from people who've never raised. Fundraising templates that treat every investor the same. Having no one inside the company to think out loud with.

**Needs from Gambit:** A map of which investors are moveable and why, term-sheet negotiation prep with a real walk-away, a premortem on the round failing and a sharper story for the deck.

**Decision triggers:** Privacy is non-negotiable, because the pipeline and the numbers are confidential. Comfortable getting an API key, and wants it fast.

**Channels:** Founder communities, X, LinkedIn, investor newsletters, peer CEO groups

**How Gambit helps:** It tracks the investor pipeline as a stakeholder map, preps each partner meeting and the term-sheet negotiation, runs a premortem on the round and keeps the fallback plan (bridge, extension, cut burn) written down before it's needed.

### Joanne, 41 — secondary school teacher leaving teaching, Melbourne

*"I've been good at this for fifteen years. I have no idea what I'm good at outside it."*

**Their world:** Joanne teaches full time and is exhausted by it, has a mortgage and a partner who's supportive but nervous, and keeps a folder of job ads that never gets sent.

**Goals:** Pick a direction worth betting on. Leave without a big drop in income. Have a plan to show at home.

**Frustrations:** Career quizzes. Coaches who cost a week's pay per session. Generic "transferable skills" advice.

**Needs from Gambit:** Someone to narrow the options, stress-test the finances and find what could go wrong before it's handed in.

**Decision triggers:** A friend sends a link. Needs the setup explained in plain steps.

**Channels:** Teacher forums, Facebook groups, LinkedIn, career-change subreddits

**How Gambit helps:** It works the options down to one direction, runs a premortem on the switch and checks the money and risk before the resignation letter goes in.

### Sam, 26 — organizer of a tenants' campaign, Chicago

*"Everyone has opinions. Nobody has a plan."*

**Their world:** Sam volunteers around a warehouse job and coordinates a dozen neighbors with conflicting ideas against a landlord who has lawyers.

**Goals:** Build a plan the group agrees on. Know who can be moved. Avoid a misstep that ends the campaign.

**Frustrations:** Meetings that go in circles. Tools built for companies.

**Needs from Gambit:** Stakeholder mapping, message framing and a record of shared decisions.

**Decision triggers:** Free, private and no account. That matters when the other side has lawyers.

**Channels:** Organizer networks, Discord, Instagram, local groups

**How Gambit helps:** It maps who holds power and who is persuadable, records decisions along with what would reverse them and drafts the message for the next meeting.

### Lena, 35 — planning to move the family from Germany to Portugal, Hamburg

*"There are fifty moving parts and three of them are people I love."*

**Their world:** Lena has a remote-friendly job with a manager who hasn't agreed yet, a partner who would need new work and two kids starting school next year. There's an 18-month window before the kids' next school change.

**Goals:** Decide if it's really happening. Get the order of things right (visa, job, schools, housing). Keep everyone on board.

**Frustrations:** Relocation checklists that ignore the family politics. Chatbot answers that are confident about visa rules and wrong.

**Needs from Gambit:** A dependency-aware plan, prep for the conversations with the manager and the partner, and an honest check on time and money.

**Decision triggers:** Privacy (family and employer details). Fine with a short guided setup.

**Channels:** Expat and relocation communities, Reddit, Facebook groups, YouTube

**How Gambit helps:** It sequences the move around what has to happen first, preps the ask to the manager and keeps one page the whole household can look at.

### Secondary: the setter-upper

Alex, 33, is a developer who finds Gambit on GitHub, reads the code, stars it and sets it up for a friend, partner or parent. Alex is how an open-source project reaches the people above, and the reason the repo has to be as clear as the app. For Alex, local-first, no backend and a strict CSP are selling points. For everyone else, those points need one plain sentence at most.

## 04 — Audience language

**Their problem:** "going in circles," "too many moving parts," "don't know where to start," "everyone has an opinion," "I keep putting it off," "can I afford this?"

**Desired outcome:** "a real plan," "make the call," "know what to do next," "stop second-guessing," "see where I stand"

**Why they chose alternatives:** "ChatGPT is right there," "my accountant handles the numbers," "a coach is too expensive," "I just keep it in my head"

**Implication:** Talk in their words: stuck, next step, the call, what could go wrong, who matters. Methods like Schwerpunkt, BATNA and PMESII stay under the hood. Gambit uses them and translates them, and the brand never leads with them.

## 05 — Who Gambit is not for

- People who want motivation, affirmations or a habit streak. Gambit will disagree with them.
- Teams wanting shared project management. Gambit holds one person's goal, on one device.
- People who want the AI to do the work (write the business plan, fill in the visa forms). Gambit helps them decide and act.

## 06 — Implications

**Messaging:** Lead with the stuck feeling and the honest-partner promise. Show goals from business, career, life and campaigning side by side, so no one reads Gambit as "for tech people." Privacy is the second message.

**Channels:** Communities for each goal type (business owners, founders, career changers, organizers, expats), plus GitHub and Hacker News for the setter-upper. Word of mouth matters most. A shareable view of the page would help.

**Voice:** Plain words, candid and warm. A good friend who happens to be a sharp strategist.

**Product:**
- Rosa and Joanne set the bar for onboarding. If they can get a key and reach a first plan without help, everyone can.
- The first session has to produce something tangible within minutes.
- Doctrine terms in the UI appear in plain language, with the technical term as a secondary label.
- Examples, placeholders and empty states should rotate across goal types, never default to software or startups.
