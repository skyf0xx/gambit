# Voice

Covers the brand and UI copy. The advisor's in-chat voice is governed by `skills/_shared/HUMANIZE.md` and `GUIDED.md`. This guide sits on top of those as a light tonal skin, never a character performance, because precise moments (confirming facts, reflecting criteria back) need plain speech.

## 01 — Voice overview

Gambit writes like a sharp friend's margin notes: short, specific and on your side without being soft. It says what it sees, asks the question you were avoiding and then gives you the next step. It doesn't hype, lecture or perform expertise. The methods show in the quality of the questions, not in the vocabulary.

**Voice essence:** Gambit sounds like a good mentor writing in your notebook: candid, practical and quietly warm.

## 02 — Tone dimensions

- **Casual ← → Formal:** casual side. Contractions, plain words, no slang. *"Let's check what this costs you in hours first."*
- **Calm ← → Energetic:** calm. No exclamation marks in UI and none in advice. *"Three things could sink this. Here's the first."*
- **Serious ← → Witty:** serious about the stakes, with occasional dry understatement. *"Bold plan. Let's see if it survives Tuesday."*
- **Accessible ← → Authoritative:** accessible first, and authoritative when needed. The technical term comes second. *"Your pressure point (strategists call it the Schwerpunkt)."*
- **Distant ← → Intimate:** close but not gushing. Uses "you." Never "we're so excited."

## 03 — Qualities

**Candid.** Gambit says the uncomfortable thing once, clearly.
- Do: "This deadline doesn't fit your available hours."
- Don't: "You might possibly want to consider revisiting the timeline!"

**Concrete.** Gambit gives dates, numbers, names and actions.
- Do: "Email two past clients by Friday."
- Don't: "Leverage your network."

**Brief.** A notebook has small pages.
- Do: one-line empty states and button labels as verbs
- Don't: paragraphs of explanation in the UI

**On your side.** Pushback always comes with a way forward.
- Do: "Weak spot here. Want to test it cheaply first?"
- Don't: "That won't work."

**Plainspoken.** Gambit translates the methods and never shows off with them.
- Do: "What would make you reverse this?"
- Don't: "Define your reversal condition per decision doctrine."

## 04 — Vocabulary

**Use:** next move, plan, page, notebook, think it through, pressure-test, what could go wrong, who matters, where you stand, decide, note, check in, on track, stuck, honest

**Avoid:** unlock, supercharge, 10X, leverage (as a verb), journey, crush it, synergy, AI-powered, revolutionary, seamless, empower, "Great question!", mission-critical, and doctrine terms without a translation

**Brand terms:**
- The goal record is **your page**. It's home, and the conversation is how it gets written.
- The user is **you**, never "user."
- Skills appear as plain verbs: *Pressure-test*, *Plan*, *Decide*, *Map people*, *Prep a conversation*, *Look back*.

## 05 — Style rules

- **Sentences:** short by default, with an occasional longer one for rhythm.
- **Punctuation:** no exclamation marks. Few dashes. Periods are fine.
- **Case:** sentence case everywhere, including buttons and headings.
- **Numbers:** always numerals. "3 next steps," "about 2 minutes."
- **Contractions:** yes.
- **Voice:** active.
- **Humor:** dry and rare. Never at the user's expense and never in error messages.

## 06 — Channel adaptations

| Channel | Adjustment | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Landing / README | Most warmth, one idea per line | "Stuck on something hard? Think it through with someone honest." |
| GitHub / HN | Technical and matter-of-fact | "Local-first PWA. No backend. Bring your own key." |
| Onboarding / key setup | Reassuring, step by step. No prices. | "This takes about 2 minutes, and the key is free." |
| Empty states | One question | "What are you trying to make happen?" |
| Errors | Plain, what happened and what to do next | "The provider rejected the key. Check it was copied in full." |

## 07 — Before and after

> Before: "Encrypted on this device and sent only to the provider you selected."
> After: "Your key stays on this device. It's only ever sent to Anthropic."

Names the actual provider and tells the user what they wanted to know.

> Before: "Get 10X better outcomes for your goals"
> After: "Make better calls on the thing that matters most right now."

Drops the unverifiable claim.

> Before: "Model id"
> After: "Model (the default is fine)"

Reduces anxiety for someone who doesn't know what a model id is.

> Before: "Schwerpunkt"
> After: "Focus. The one place effort counts most."

Translates the term and keeps its meaning.

## 08 — Never say

- Hype: "game-changing," "10X," "unlock your potential"
- Fake enthusiasm: "Awesome!", "Love that!", "Great question!"
- Guilt: "You haven't checked in for 5 days!"
- Talk of the AI's feelings: "I'm so excited to help"
- Unexplained jargon in UI labels
