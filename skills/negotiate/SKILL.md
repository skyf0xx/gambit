# Skill: negotiate

**Trigger**: A conversation is coming where the other side has to agree to something.
Permission, money, access, a venue, a partnership, a division of work, a de-escalation
with someone who's been blocking.

Also use after `stakeholders` identifies a high-power actor who needs moving — this is
how the conversation gets prepped.

**Purpose**: Walk in prepared. `comms` shapes what you broadcast; this prepares a two-way
exchange where the other side has leverage and their own goals.

Most people prepare by rehearsing their argument. That is the least useful preparation
available. What actually determines the outcome is knowing what they want, knowing what
you'll do if there's no deal, and knowing in advance what you'll trade — decided while
calm, not in the room.

---

## Voice & Tone

Practical and unsentimental, without being adversarial. Most negotiations that matter here
are with people the user will deal with again — a council officer, a venue manager, a
fellow organiser. Winning a single exchange at the cost of the relationship is usually a
bad trade, and should be named when it's on the table.

Firm on substance. Do not coach the user into conceding early to be agreeable — that's the
most common failure, and it's expensive.

---

## Execution Sequence

### 1. Load Context

Read `GOAL.md` — goal, `## Plan`, `## People`, `## Stakeholders` if it exists. If the
counterparty appears in `## Stakeholders`, pull their recorded interest rather than
re-deriving it.

### 2. Define What You Actually Need

Separate, precisely:

```
MUST HAVE: [without this, no deal is worth doing]
WANT: [valuable, tradeable]
NICE: [give away deliberately, to buy something]
```

If "must have" has more than two items, it's probably a wish list. Test each: would you
genuinely walk away without it? If not, it's a want.

### 3. Your BATNA

The single most important input, and the most commonly skipped:

```
BATNA — Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement
  If this conversation produces nothing, I will: [the specific fallback]
  How good is that, honestly: strong | workable | poor
  Can it be improved before the conversation? [what would strengthen it]
```

Your BATNA sets your real floor. A weak BATNA doesn't mean concede — it means know you're
exposed, and improve it beforehand if there's time. An hour spent strengthening the
alternative usually beats an hour spent rehearsing arguments.

Never disclose a weak BATNA. Never bluff a strong one you don't have — it gets tested, and
being caught costs more than the deal.

### 4. Their Side

Work it as carefully as your own. Guessing here is where negotiations are lost:

```
THEIR INTERESTS: [what they actually want — not their stated position]
THEIR CONSTRAINTS: [what they can't do, regardless of willingness — policy, budget, a boss,
                    precedent, optics]
THEIR BATNA: [what happens for them if there's no deal — are they under pressure or not?]
WHO ACTUALLY DECIDES: [is this person the decision-maker, or carrying it to someone?]
```

That last line changes the whole approach. If they're a messenger, the goal is to make it
easy for them to advocate internally — which means giving them something to say, not
winning an argument with them.

Where an interest or constraint is a guess, mark it and offer `research`.

### 5. Find the Zone

```
ZOPA — Zone Of Possible Agreement
  Your floor: [worst acceptable]
  Their likely floor: [estimated]
  Overlap: exists | doesn't appear to | unknown
```

If there's no overlap, say so before the conversation. The options then are: change what's
being negotiated (add an issue, extend the timeframe, split the deal), improve your BATNA,
or don't have the meeting. Discovering it in the room wastes the meeting and hardens
positions.

### 6. Rank Concessions by Cost

Decide this in advance, in writing:

```
CONCESSION LADDER — give in this order
  1. [item] — costs me: [low] — worth to them: [high]   ← trade these first
  2. [item] — costs me: [medium] — worth to them: [medium]
  3. [item] — costs me: [high] — only for: [what you require in return]

NEVER: [what you will not trade, at any price]
```

The best trades are cheap to you and valuable to them — they exist because the two sides
value things differently, and they're invisible unless you've mapped both interest sets.

Every concession past the first should be conditional: "I can do X if you can do Y."
Unilateral concessions teach the other side to wait.

### 7. Openings, Anchors, and Traps

```
OPEN WITH: [your opening position — ambitious but justifiable]
JUSTIFICATION: [the standard or precedent it rests on — anchors need a reason to stick]
IF THEY ANCHOR FIRST: [how to reset without rejecting outright]

TRAPS TO EXPECT
  [tactic] — [what it looks like] — [response]
```

Common ones worth prepping for: artificial deadline pressure, "that's just our policy",
escalating demands after apparent agreement, splitting the difference from an extreme
anchor, and the deal that changes when it's written down.

### 8. Elicit

```
Before you go in:

  - What are you most afraid they'll say?
  - What would you accept right now if they offered it — honestly?
```

The second question exposes a real floor that's often well above the stated one. If the
user would accept something today that sits below their stated "must have", the must-have
list is wrong, and better to find that out now than mid-conversation.

The first question usually surfaces the argument they feel weakest against — which is the
one worth preparing.

### 9. Set the Walk-Away Rule

Written in advance, while calm:

```
I WALK IF: [specific, observable condition]
I ALSO WALK IF: [the relationship or process condition — bad faith, moving terms]
```

Deciding to walk is nearly impossible in the room, under social pressure, with sunk time
behind you. Deciding it beforehand is the only reliable way it happens.

### 10. Update GOAL.md and Name the Next Step

Log the negotiation, what was agreed, and any commitment the user made — a commitment
given in a conversation and not recorded is one nobody can hold either side to.

If it produced an agreement with obligations, hand to `plan` to sequence them.

```
Next: [strengthen the BATNA | confirm a guessed interest → research |
       draft the opening → comms | after the conversation → review]
```
