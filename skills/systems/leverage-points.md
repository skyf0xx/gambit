# Leverage points

Donella Meadows, *Thinking in Systems: A Primer* (2008), building on her 1997 essay
"Leverage Points: Places to Intervene in a System". Her twelve places to intervene,
weakest first. The method is hers; the wording and examples here are Gambit's.

| # | Where to intervene | What it looks like in a goal |
|---|---|---|
| 12 | Numbers: constants, budgets, targets | more hours, a bigger ad spend, a higher quota |
| 11 | Buffers: the size of stocks held against shocks | savings, spare volunteers, slack in the timeline |
| 10 | Physical structure: how things are laid out and connected | where the event is held, which tools are used |
| 9 | Delays: how long feedback takes to arrive | learning a result in a day instead of a month |
| 8 | Balancing loops: the checks that pull a system back | a review step, a cap, a regular check-in |
| 7 | Reinforcing loops: what feeds on itself | referrals, word of mouth, compounding savings |
| 6 | Information flows: who can see what | publishing a number, telling the decision-maker who is affected |
| 5 | Rules: incentives, constraints, who may do what | a policy change, a contract term, a by-law |
| 4 | Self-organisation: the power to change the system's own structure | members who can start their own chapters |
| 3 | Goals: what the system is trying to do | changing what a body is judged on |
| 2 | Paradigms: the shared beliefs the system rests on | what everyone takes as given about how this works |
| 1 | Transcending paradigms | holding no one model as the whole truth |

## Using it

- Most plans push on numbers (12) because they are easy to see and argue about. They
  rarely shift the outcome on their own: the structure soon pulls the system back.
- Higher levels move more, and are harder to reach and slower to show. A goal of a few
  weeks seldom changes a paradigm; it can often change an information flow or a rule.
- Look for the highest level the user can actually reach with their own hours and
  people. A level-6 move the user can make this week beats a level-3 move they can't.
- Meadows warns that people often push a high-leverage point in the wrong direction.
  Say which way the move pushes, not only where.
